Naor-Shamir (2, 2) Visual Threshold Scheme & Technical Overview

Invented by Moni Naor and Adi Shamir (1994), Visual Cryptography is a perfectly secure secret sharing scheme:

  • 2x2 Subpixel Expansion: Every pixel in the secret image is mapped to a \(2 \times 2\) block of subpixels in Share 1 and Share 2.
  • White Pixel Representation: Share 1 and Share 2 are assigned the exact same random subpixel pattern. When overlaid, 2 subpixels are black and 2 are white, allowing 50% light transmittance (appears light/white).
  • Black Pixel Representation: Share 1 and Share 2 are assigned complementary subpixel patterns. When overlaid, all 4 subpixels turn black, blocking 100% of light transmittance (appears solid black).
  • PIN-Authenticated Secret Sharing: By seeding the cryptographically secure PRNG with the shared secret PIN, Share 1 can be regenerated on-demand by the receiver. Thus, only Share 2 needs to be transmitted, and possession of the PIN is strictly required to decrypt.
  • Information-Theoretic Security: Even with unlimited supercomputer power, an attacker holding only Share 2 has a strictly 50/50 probability distribution for every pixel, guaranteeing mathematical secrecy.
